About Shanmuga Organics

எங்களாவை அறியுங்கள்

A family shop and a small processing unit in Erode, Tamil Nadu, making wood-pressed oils, heritage rice, millets and herbal care since 2009. One press, one product, one street — here is how the shop grew, including the years that went badly.

We started because good oil got hard to find

In 2009 my father was buying groundnut oil for the house and could not find a single brand that was actually pressed rather than solvent-extracted. So he bought one second-hand wooden chekku, pressed enough for the family, and neighbours started asking for a litre.

Sixteen years later the unit runs six presses, a small rice mill, a masala grinding room and a cold room for ghee. The rule has not changed: if we would not cook with it at home, it does not go on the shelf.

What we actually do ourselves

  • Press all six oils in wooden chekku, filter by settling, never by chemicals
  • Mill and single-polish our own rice so the bran layer survives
  • Roast and grind health mixes, podis and sambar powder in weekly batches
  • Cut, cure and wrap the soaps by hand
  • Clarify ghee from cream we collect from two nearby dairies

What we buy in

Honey comes from a forest-collector co-operative in the Nilgiris. Nattu sakkarai is made by a jaggery unit in Karur. We say so on those pages, because pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

How we work

எங்கள் வழிமுறை

Buy direct, pay on the spot

நேரடி கொள்முதல்

No commission agent between us and the farm. We weigh in front of the farmer, agree a rate against that week's market, and pay before the load leaves.

Press slow, keep it cool

மெதுவான செக்கு முறை

A wooden chekku turns at around 20 rpm. Oil never crosses 40°C, so the aroma and the vitamin E survive. It is slower and the yield is lower — that is the trade.

Make small, sell fresh

சிறிய அளவில் தயாரிப்பு

We grind podis weekly and press oil to order where we can. Nothing sits in a warehouse for a season waiting for a preservative to keep it alive.

The chekku in the back room

The first press sat in what is now our packing room. It was bought used from a unit in Kangeyam that was closing down, and it groaned. Fourteen kilos of groundnut went in and about four and a half litres of oil came out — a terrible yield by refinery standards, and exactly what a slow press gives you.

For the first two years we sold only groundnut oil, only in refilled glass bottles, only to people who walked in. Rice came later, when a farmer in Bhavani offered us his Ponni paddy because the mandi rate that season insulted him.

Sixteen years, roughly

காலவரிசை

2009One press, one product

A second-hand wooden chekku and groundnut oil sold in refilled bottles to neighbours on Dharapuram Road.

2011Rice enters the shop

A Bhavani farmer's Ponni paddy, single-polished on a rented mill. We bought the whole harvest and hoped it would sell. It took nine months.

2013Sesame and coconut

Two more presses. Nallennai for the Tamil kitchen and coconut oil for hair and cooking both, which is how it has always been used here.

2015The bad monsoon

A failed north-east monsoon wiped out the groundnut we had contracted for. We paid the farmers anyway, borrowed against the shop, and lost most of a year.

2017Millets and the grinding room

Kambu, thinai, ragi and varagu from Tiruppur dryland farms, plus a masala room for health mix, sambar podi and idli podi.

2019Soap, made by hand

Multani mitti, neem and turmeric bars cured on wooden racks for four weeks. Our first product that had nothing to do with food.

2021Wholesale opens up

Restaurants and provision stores started buying 15 kg tins. We added the bulk price list and stopped pretending we were only a retail counter.

2024Fifty farms, one ledger

Fifty-two supply partners across Erode, Karur, Tiruppur and the Nilgiris — each paid at the gate, each written into a book we can show you.

Three things we got wrong

We over-polished the rice for a while. Customers complained that our Ponni looked dull next to supermarket rice, so in 2012 we polished it harder. It sold better and it was worse food. We reverted in 2014 and explained why on a board at the counter, which we should have done in the first place.

We used the word "organic" too loosely. Early labels said organic on products that came from farms with no certification and no verified practice. We now say where something came from and how it was grown, and we leave the claim to the reader.

We shipped ghee in summer without insulation. Three 2023 orders arrived as liquid in a leaking jar. We now pack ghee in a sealed tin inside a lined box, and we still refund the ones that go wrong.

Licences and testing

We hold FSSAI licence 12421008000341 for the manufacturing unit and GSTIN 33ABCDE1234F1Z5. Oil is sent for a free-fatty-acid and peroxide check every quarter; rice and millets go for a pesticide-residue screen twice a year. Ask for the latest report and we will send the PDF.

Where we are honest about limits

We are not a certified-organic operator for every line. Some farmers we buy from follow organic practice without paperwork, and a few products — sunflower oil, wheat flour — come from conventional farms. Those pages say so plainly rather than borrowing the word "organic" for everything.
